I

THE CODEX

Who is typing.

I build interfaces for things that can hurt you.

For the last two years I have been the HMI guy at Kaban Makina — the person who stands between an operator's fingers and a servo-driven blade. Full-screen touch programs, written in C#, running on machines that cut, punch, bend and notch metal all day. If the screen lies, the part is scrap. If the screen freezes, the line stops. So the screen does not lie and it does not freeze.

Before the steel there was the tape. Two years at a forex house, writing C# for systems that had to be awake when the market was: live price feeds, buy and sell order routing, execution and settlement flows, all of it split into services so one bad component could not take the floor down with it.

Different rooms, identical religion: deterministic behaviour, honest state, and no surprises at 3 a.m.

  • 001The operator is never wrong. The UI is.
  • 002A number on a screen is a promise. Keep it.
  • 003Log like you will be woken up about it.
  • 004Ship it, then stand next to it while it runs.
